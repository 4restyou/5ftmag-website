FILM OPTIK OLDSCHOOL INOVISCOAT

Same emulsion, four namesWhat Optik OptiColour 200 really is

US indie brand Optik Oldschool has released a new color film, OptiColour 200. The emulsion inside is a familiar one, though: the same InovisCoat line as ORWO Wolfen NC200 and KONO Color 200. Optik changed exactly one thing, the base color.

This article was translated from Korean with AI. Read the original in Korean →

Another new color film is out. On the surface, anyway. Open the box and you find an emulsion that is already on the market. Once again, the small realities of indie color film are laid bare.

A new film from Optik Oldschool

Optik Oldschool is a small American film brand. It doesn't own a factory. It is closer to a company that takes film made elsewhere, adjusts it to its own taste and sells it in its own box. Its new release is OptiColour 200, an ISO 200, daylight, C-41, 36 EXP 35mm color negative film. The first shipments were 120 (medium format), starting in June 2025 at around €9.90, and the 35mm 36 exp box is rolling out in earnest in 2026.

Inside: ORWO Wolfen NC200

The box design is new, but the emulsion is already on the market. It is a color negative made by InovisCoat GmbH, which is to say ORWO Wolfen NC200.

First, the names need sorting out. ORWO is short for "ORiginal WOlfen," a brand that began at a factory in Bitterfeld-Wolfen in East Germany. The original ORWO company went bankrupt in 1994, and the brand and some of its assets passed through several hands before ending up at InovisCoat GmbH in Monheim am Rhein, Germany. InovisCoat is a small coating maker with know-how in color emulsion coating. In 2020, British businessman Jake Seal bought both InovisCoat and FilmoTec, a cine film company with East German roots, and got the current ORWO Wolfen line running in earnest.

In other words, the ORWO Wolfen color films on the market today are not direct descendants of the old East German ORWO factory. They are new coatings from InovisCoat. The same emulsion from the same coating line changes only its name depending on whose box it goes into.

One emulsion, four names

Right now the same InovisCoat NC200 emulsion is sold under at least four names.

  • ORWO Wolfen NC200, the original name. Its own brand, with the base color unchanged.
  • KONO! Color 200, an OEM box from the Austrian indie label KONO.
  • Lomochrome Classicolor 200, the same emulsion sold by Lomography as part of its lineup.
  • Optik OptiColour 200, the American label that just joined the list, released with a single change.

Expose these four rolls under the same conditions, develop them in the same chemistry, and you get the same result. Only the box color and the price differ.

The one thing Optik changed: the base color

So what did Optik change? It names one difference: the base color.

The original InovisCoat NC200 comes on a slightly greenish base. Users have long said that this green shows up as a color cast when scanning and makes more work in post. Optik had the film coated on the orange mask base used by ordinary color negative films instead. The result is a roll that fits better with the auto white balance and inversion profiles of a standard C-41 workflow.

Chemically it is the same emulsion. What changes is how much work scanning and editing take. Some people use the original NC200 precisely because they like the green base (they like the tones they get by working it themselves), and others find it a nuisance. Optik is aiming at the second group.

Specs, price and release

  • Speed, ISO 200 (24 DIN), daylight balanced
  • Process, standard C-41
  • Formats, 35mm 36 EXP, 120 (medium format)
  • 120 release, from June 2025 at about €9.90 (per roll, about 11 dollars)
  • 35mm release, wide distribution in 2026
  • Distribution in Korea, not confirmed. As of June 2026 there is no official Korean channel

The price is close to average for indie color negative. It lands at a moment when list prices for Kodak Gold 200 and Portra 400 have gone up and indie color looks attractive again on price.

How the ORWO Wolfen color line has evolved

While we are sorting out what OptiColour is, it helps to look at the current ORWO Wolfen color line too. The same InovisCoat coating line has been improving step by step.

  • NC500 (ISO 400), the line's first color film, in 2022. Its coarse grain and low saturation divided opinion. The traces of its cine film lineage were strong.
  • NC400 (ISO 400), a revision that refined NC500's grain and lifted saturation a little. Its colors came closer to a normal color negative.
  • NC200 (ISO 200), the newest. According to InovisCoat, the green channel is a step more stable and the dynamic range is wider. It has become the easiest all-round roll for portraits and landscapes.

Interestingly, the successor to NC400 is not a new ISO 400 but NC200, a stop slower. That seems to reflect how indie color film is mostly used: outdoors in daylight, for landscapes and portraits. It is also why OptiColour chose to start at 200.

The OEM structure behind indie color film

Step back and the reality of color negative film comes into view again. The companies in the world that design and make new color film from scratch can be counted on one hand: Kodak, Fujifilm, ORWO Wolfen / InovisCoat, and a few small outfits that cut cine film down for still cameras. That's it.

Of these, InovisCoat is a small coating company that runs its own brand and OEM work at the same time. Brands like KONO!, Lomography and Optik have no coating lines of their own, so they end up queuing for the same InovisCoat line. There they adjust only the final touches, such as base color, box design and price, to suit themselves. That is how the same emulsion naturally ends up on the shelf under four names.

None of this is new. In the 1990s some Fuji films were sold under other companies' labels, and AGFA color went a similar way. The growing number of indie color films today does not really mean the market has grown. It means more labels are lining up at the same small coating line.

So before buying OptiColour 200, here is a question worth asking. It's NC200 either way, so which suits your workflow better, the green base or the orange one? Both are NC200, and that doesn't change. Whether the price gap between labels is worth the difference, you'll only know after shooting a roll of each.

Summary

  • Optik OptiColour 200 is a new color negative from US indie brand Optik Oldschool. ISO 200, daylight, C-41.
  • The emulsion inside is the same as InovisCoat's ORWO Wolfen NC200, and the same line as KONO! Color 200 and Lomochrome Classicolor 200.
  • Optik changed one thing, the base color. It swapped the original green base for an orange mask to make it easier to scan.
  • Release: 120 from June 2025 (around €9.90), 35mm 36 EXP in wide distribution in 2026.
  • The ORWO Wolfen color line has gone NC500 → NC400 → NC200. The successor settled on a slower ISO, not a faster one.
  • Many indie color films are tied to the same InovisCoat coating line. A different label does not mean different chemistry.
  • Official distribution in Korea is unconfirmed as of June 2026.
