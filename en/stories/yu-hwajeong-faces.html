Yu Hwa-jeong's 《Faces of All Things》Faces of people, animals and places

At Comeamico Gallery from October 21 to 27. A first solo show built from eight years of film, with a first photo-essay book to match.

5ft.mag Editors 2026.10.02

This article was translated from Korean with AI. Read the original in Korean →

Yu Hwa-jeong's first solo exhibition, 《Faces of All Things》 (얼굴들), opens on Wednesday, October 21. It runs seven days, through Tuesday the 27th, at Comeamico Gallery in Seongsan-dong, Mapo-gu, Seoul. The poster also carries a span of years: 2019 to 2026.

Eight years of moments

According to the artist's announcement on Threads, this is a first show built from eight years of film. It ties together, in photographs and short texts, the people, animals and landscapes she met while moving from place to place, and the moments that happened in between. The exhibition statement says it collects "the things that stay with you long after, rather than any special event." The list runs from animals met on a beach and conversations struck up by chance to views on the road and scenes from an ordinary day.

The faces in the title do not stop at people. The statement puts the faces of animals, the sea, cities and the places she stayed on the same line. The poster makes the point first. The center of the frame belongs to a pony coming toward the wire, with out-of-focus roses hanging in front.

The three photographs released so far carry the same idea. The goat with its hooves on the wire looks straight at the lens. The harnessed goat and the child head the same way. On the tree-lined street, the cockatoo sweeping into the edge of the frame catches the eye before the two people do. None of them decides in advance who the subject is.

A first photo-essay book

Timed to the show, she plans to self-publish her first photo-essay book. The released mockup spreads either set a photograph against a page of text or lay two photographs side by side. The texts run in Korean and English, one above the other.

One page carries a short piece that opens with "I like old ladies." The English version on the same page reads:

I like talking to old ladies especially the ones I meet while traveling. I always remember our conversations. Their advice, complaints, even their nagging, I enjoy it all. They seem to be natural storytellers. And they seem to like me too. Maybe they're just happy to find someone who will listen.

Below the text are the names Karen & Keisha. The photograph opposite shows a person sitting in a chair in the shade of a tree, with a brown dog standing in front.

Other pages carry a short place name or year. The cat on a red leash on a street in Havana is captioned "Havana, Cuba," and the children on the lawn "2024, VIC." The format, page count, price and where to buy it have not been announced. Whether it will be on hand at the gallery first is something to watch for in the artist's own updates.

The artist will be there

She has said she plans to stay at the gallery for the run of the show. Comeamico Gallery is a café and exhibition space in an alley of Seongsan-dong, between Yeonnam-dong and Mangwon-dong. You can ask the person who took a photograph about the story behind it. Which face will you end up standing in front of the longest?
