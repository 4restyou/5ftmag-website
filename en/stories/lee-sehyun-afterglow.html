Lee Se-hyun's 《Afterglow》Light That Lingers Where It Went Out

At SPACE LEESEEN through September 20. Red and blue light cast on bullet marks, mine tunnels and barbed-wire fences.

5ft.mag Editors 2026.09.12

This article was translated from Korean with AI. Read the original in Korean →

Lee Se-hyun's photography exhibition 《Afterglow》 (잔광, 殘光) opened on September 8. It closes on Sunday the 20th, which leaves eight days. The venue is SPACE LEESEEN.

Shot with cast light

The artist set up his camera in six places: bullet marks lodged in the concrete at Nogeun-ri, the tunnels of the Gyeongsan cobalt mine, the old operating room of the Gwangju Red Cross Hospital, the Gwangcheon Citizens' Apartments, military fences along the east coast, and ground in Uiseong where a wildfire passed through. At these places, anywhere from a year to more than seventy years have gone by since the events.

Lee cast red and blue artificial light over them himself and photographed the result. Visit by day and the concrete is just concrete, the tunnel just a tunnel. Add light and the surface changes. The marks bullets left and the soot take on color and come up again. All the works on the walls belong to the series 「Blue Face, Red Night」 (푸른 낯, 붉은 밤). The word 「nat」 (낯) in the Korean title means face.

Three strands

The exhibition is divided into three parts. The first is the darkness of the bullet marks and the mine tunnels. The second is the things left behind at the Gwangju Red Cross Hospital and the Gwangcheon Citizens' Apartments. The third is the wreckage in Uiseong and the fences on the east coast.

The first two concern events from long ago. The last happened just this past spring. Scenes separated by wide gaps in time hang in one room. The exhibition statement puts it in a single short line: "All history leads to the present."

Red light and blue light

Lee has built this series by seeking out places that still carry the scars of modern Korean history. Last year's Gwangju Young Artists Invitational at the Gwangju Museum of Art's Ha Jung-woong Museum of Art, 《Lee Se-hyun: Blue Face, Red Night》 (이세현 : 푸른 낯, 붉은 밤), was a midpoint review. The title sets a face gone blue with fear against red light in the dark. Blue stands for loss and mourning, red for what remains of violence and resistance.

The same series also includes photographs of the Armed Forces Gwangju Hospital and the 505 Security Unit. Did he settle on the colors first and then choose the places, or did the places call up the colors? That is for each visitor to work out in the gallery. If you find a photograph where the two colors meet in one frame, start there and take your time.
