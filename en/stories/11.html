Grain isn't noise

10 films etched in light on film. Here's a first look at five of the movies we covered in the 〈Feature〉 section of Vol.02.

Film Social Club 2026.02.08

This article was translated from Korean with AI. Read the original in Korean →

"Digital perfection has no feeling."

Digital signals run the world now, and the latest sensors copy whatever is in front of them perfectly, without a speck of noise. But in a world where fast and efficient has become the right answer, there are still filmmakers who load heavy film magazines into their cameras instead of cold memory cards.

There's a kind of beauty, half chance and half inevitability, that only a chemical reaction can make and that smooth images chopped into pixels never hold. Film grain, boiling up at random, makes the frame tremble like a character's ragged breathing, and the rough colors are what finally give a story its texture.

For the 〈Feature〉 section of Vol.02, 5ft magazine picked 10 movies shot on film. From the chilly winter air of 〈Carol〉 to the blazing summer sun of 〈The Florida Project〉, the tungsten nights of 〈Chungking Express〉 and the heavy black-and-white IMAX film of 〈Oppenheimer〉. Ten movies, and the ways the masters handle light, brought into your own viewfinder.

Five to start with

Let's look at the light in five of the ten first. For each one: the film stock it was shot on, a stock you can buy that gets closest to its look, and a tip for getting that mood into your own camera.

No.01 · Romance / Period Drama

Carol

Carol · 2015

My angel, flung out of space.

New York in the 1950s. A department store clerk and an alluring customer fall into a fated, forbidden love. Feelings rising through cold winter air, caught delicately on 16mm.

Shot on Kodak Vision3 500T 7219, 250D 7207, 50D 7203, 200T 7213
Our pick Lomography 800: comes closest to the rough grain of 16mm and its vintage color with a reddish cast.
Shooting tip Look at your subject through 'glass,' like the inside of a car in the rain or a fogged-up window. You'll get dreamy refractions, like a Saul Leiter photograph.
No.03 · Drama / Coming-of-age

The Florida Project

The Florida Project · 2017

Right next to the most magical place, the most real of lives.

The world as seen by six-year-old Moonee, who lives in a run-down motel next to Disney World. Kids' innocence and the harsh reality of poverty, told in bold, pop-art color contrasts.

Shot on Kodak Vision3 250D 7207, 50D 7203
Our pick Kodak Portra 160: in bright exposures it gives soft, airy pastel colors. For when you want to shoot 'fairy-tale reality.'
Shooting tip Use front light at noon on a clear day (shoot with the sun at your back). Deep shadows, vivid color: that's how you get a pop-art look.
No.05 · Romance / Summer

Call Me by Your Name

Call Me by Your Name · 2017

Call me by your name and I'll call you by mine.

Italy, 1983. The record of a burning first love between a 17-year-old boy and a 24-year-old young man. The sunlight and lazy air of that summer, all of it captured on film.

Shot on Kodak Vision3 500T 5219
Our pick Kodak Gold 200: its warm golden tone is the closest thing to midsummer Italian sun. The film that turns an everyday summer into a movie.
Shooting tip Go for the slanting light of late afternoon. A face in profile, long shadows, light breaking through leaves. That's a scene where time slows down.
No.08 · Romance / Hong Kong

Chungking Express

Chungking Express · 1994

If love has an expiry date, I want it to be ten thousand years.

Hong Kong at night under bright neon, and the loneliness and romance of four people who brush past and miss each other. Restless handheld camerawork and one-of-a-kind color paint the city's night streets.

Shot on Agfa XT 320 (discontinued)
Our pick Cinestill 800T: a tungsten cine film adapted for still cameras. It nails the cold, mysterious green tones of Chungking Express and the way night lights bloom. The main film of Vol.02.
Shooting tip Don't be scared of shooting at night. Use street signs and shop-window lights as a backdrop and try a cool-toned portrait.
No.10 · Biopic / Black & White

Oppenheimer

Oppenheimer · 2023

Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds.

A biopic about the glory and guilt of the genius scientist who led the development of the atomic bomb. Moving between black and white and color, it was the first film ever shot on IMAX black-and-white film, and it pulls you all the way in.

Shot on Kodak Vision3 250D 5207, 500T 5219, Kodak Double-X 5222 (B&W)
Our pick Cinestill bwXX (Double-X): exactly the same kind of black-and-white film as the Double-X used in the movie. You get those thick blacks and rough texture as they are.
Shooting tip Give black and white a try. Where the color drops away, the texture of your subject and the play of light and shadow come through. The more contrast, the more dramatic.

The other five are in the magazine

The rough grain of a kid skating through LA streets in the '90s, a sticky love in Buenos Aires, a road movie across the desert, warm Kodak in a cold courtroom, Wes Anderson's obsessive symmetry. The stories of five more movies, each etched in its own light, are in Vol.02.

  • 2018
    Mid90s LA in the '90s. A 13-year-old grows up by crashing into the world on a skateboard. 4:3 aspect ratio and rough grain.
  • 1997
    Happy Together Lovers in Argentina who keep breaking up and getting back together. Wong Kar-wai's sensual imagery and uneasy angles.
  • 1984
    Paris, Texas A man walks out of the desert and sets off to find his memories. A road movie of bleak landscapes and neon color like no other.
  • 2019
    Marriage Story A couple who, through divorce, come to understand what their relationship really was. The warm texture of Kodak film.
  • 2023
    Asteroid City Wes Anderson's obsessive symmetrical compositions and pastel colors. An imaginary desert town in 1955.

The film stock behind each movie, our picks and detailed shooting tips for getting the mood into your camera are all in the 〈Feature〉 section of 5ft magazine Vol.02.
